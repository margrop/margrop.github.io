41 Free Online Logic Puzzles: A Quick Guide to the puzzle-*.com Series

Published: 2026-10-18 · Views --
益智游戏 逻辑谜题 数独 网站推荐 puzzle

Looking for brain teasers that need no download and no account—just a browser tab? Here is a series I have had bookmarked for a long time: puzzle-*.com.

It is not one site but a family of sites, one domain per puzzle: puzzle-sudoku.com is Sudoku, puzzle-nonograms.com is Nonograms, puzzle-loop.com is Slitherlink, and so on—41 in total. A row of banners at the bottom of every page lets you jump around the whole series.

Why it is good

  • Free, nothing to install: it runs in the browser, works with touch on phones, and saves your progress locally. There are some ads on the page.
  • Rules on one screen: each puzzle’s rules sit right below the board, and most come with a video tutorial.
  • A full difficulty ladder: the same puzzle usually comes in sizes from 5×5 to 20×20 or larger, and in easy, normal and hard levels.
  • Daily, weekly and monthly specials: besides random boards there are fixed special puzzles, with a Hall of Fame ranking solve times.
  • Shared controls: left-click fills or shades, right-click marks an × or a dot to rule a cell out. Most shading puzzles let you click and drag across several cells.

All 41 puzzles at a glance

The 41 puzzles fall into six groups by mechanic. The rules below are condensed from each site’s official description.

🔢 Fill in numbers: the Sudoku family

Puzzle Rules
Sudoku Fill a 9×9 grid with 1–9 so no digit repeats in any row, column or 3×3 box
Jigsaw Sudoku Sudoku rules, but the boxes have irregular shapes
Killer Sudoku Sudoku plus cages: digits in each dotted cage add up to its corner clue and do not repeat
Kakuro Fill white cells with 1–9; a clue in a black cell is the sum of the run to its right or below, with no repeats in a run
Skyscrapers Place towers of height 1–N with no repeats per row or column; edge clues say how many towers are visible from that side
Futoshiki Fill 1–N with no repeats per row or column, satisfying the greater-than and less-than signs
Renzoku Fill 1–N with no repeats; a dot between two cells means their numbers are consecutive, no dot means they are not

⬛ Shade cells

Puzzle Rules
Hitori Shade cells so no unshaded number repeats in a row or column; shaded cells never touch orthogonally and unshaded cells stay connected
Heyawake Numbered regions hold that many black cells; black cells never touch orthogonally, white cells stay connected, and a straight run of white cells crosses at most two regions
Nurikabe Each number is an island of that many white cells, one number per island; the black “stream” is connected and has no 2×2 blocks
Kurodoko Each number counts the white cells visible from it in four directions, itself included; black cells never touch orthogonally and white cells stay connected
Norinori Shade exactly 2 cells per region; every shaded cell is part of a 1×2 domino, and dominoes touch only diagonally
LITS Place one L, I, T or S tetromino per region; identical shapes never share an edge, all shaded cells connect, and no 2×2 blocks
Tapa Clues list the lengths of black runs among the 8 surrounding cells; all black cells connect, with no 2×2 blocks
Yin-Yang Color every cell black or white so each color forms one connected group, with no 2×2 of the same color
Nonograms Clues beside each row and column give the lengths of its black runs; shade them to reveal a picture
Mosaic A Minesweeper variant: each number counts the black cells in the 3×3 area around it, itself included
Kakurasu The 1st cell in a row is worth 1, the 2nd worth 2, and so on; shaded cells must sum to the clue at the end of each row and column
Binairo Place black and white circles: equal counts per row and column, no three of a color in a line, and no two identical rows or columns
Binairo+ Binairo with signs (LinkedIn’s Tango): “=” means same color, “×” means opposite colors

➰ Draw lines

Puzzle Rules
Slitherlink Connect dots into a single loop with no crossings or branches; each number says how many of its four sides are used
Masyu Draw a loop through every circle: go straight through white ones and turn next to them; turn on black ones and go straight on both sides
Shingoki Like Masyu—straight through white, turn on black—and the number in a circle is the total length of its two straight arms
Pipes Rotate the tiles so all pipes join into one network with no closed loops
Hashi Build straight bridges between numbered islands, at most two per pair, never crossing; each island’s bridges match its number and all islands connect
Slant Draw a diagonal in every cell; numbers at corners say how many diagonals meet there, and the lines may not form a loop
Stitches Join every block to each neighboring block with one stitch across their border, never sharing a hole; edge clues count the holes per row and column

📍 Place objects

Puzzle Rules
Minesweeper Classic Minesweeper: numbers count the mines in the 8 surrounding cells; find them all
Light Up Place bulbs that light every white cell along rows and columns up to a black cell; bulbs may not light each other, and numbered black cells count adjacent bulbs
Tents Pair each tree with one tent next to it orthogonally; tents never touch, even diagonally, and edge clues count the tents per row and column
Battleships Find the fleet shown in the legend; ships never touch, even diagonally, and edge clues count occupied cells per row and column
Star Battle Place 1 (or N) stars in every row, column and region, with no two stars touching even diagonally (LinkedIn’s Queens)
Lollipops Complete lollipops—a circle plus a one-cell stick—so different lollipops never share an edge and same-type parts in a row or column are separated by another part
Aquarium Fill aquariums with water at one level across each tank’s full width; edge clues count the filled cells per row and column
Thermometers Fill thermometers with mercury from the bulb with no gaps; edge clues count the filled cells per row and column
Shakashaka Place black right triangles in white cells so the remaining white areas are all rectangles (upright or at 45°); numbered black cells count adjacent triangles

✂️ Divide the grid

Puzzle Rules
Shikaku Cut the grid into rectangles, each holding exactly one number equal to its area
Galaxies Split the grid into galaxies, each with exactly one dot and unchanged when rotated 180° around it
Dominosa Pair the numbers into dominoes so every number pair appears exactly once

♟️ Others

Puzzle Rules
Chess Puzzles Chess pieces move normally, but every move must capture (kings included) until one piece is left
Word search games Boggle: chain adjacent letters into as many words as you can; longer words score more

A beginner path

41 puzzles sounds like a lot, but the rules overlap heavily. Try them in this order, starting at 5×5 or the easy level:

  1. Sudoku → Killer Sudoku → Kakuro: start with familiar number placement, then add sum constraints.
  2. Minesweeper → Mosaic → Nonograms: all about deducing black cells from numbers—and Nonograms reward you with a pixel picture.
  3. Hitori → Nurikabe → Heyawake: practice the two most common constraints, “black cells don’t touch” and “white cells stay connected”.
  4. Slitherlink → Masyu → Shingoki: loop puzzles, where the key idea is that every dot has either 0 or 2 lines.
  5. Star Battle and Binairo+: if you play LinkedIn’s Queens and Tango, you can jump straight to bigger boards.

A few tips:

  • Use right-click marks: put an × on cells you have ruled out; it saves a lot of re-checking.
  • Stuck? Watch the tutorial: puzzles like Yin-Yang and LITS rely on a few lemmas covered in the official videos, without which even easy boards feel hard.
  • Don’t chase the clock: the Hall of Fame is fierce; the fun is solving it yourself.

The series is also in my favorites for quick access.
